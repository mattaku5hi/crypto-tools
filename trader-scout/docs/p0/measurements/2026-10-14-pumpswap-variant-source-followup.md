# PumpSwap dated-source follow-up: legacy wire variants and cashback tail

As of 2026-10-14, the first-party material reviewed here does not close the captured instruction-wire gaps for 6SFB/7rWY or the 16-byte F4 event suffix. The corrected replay proves the event payloads and pool-vault accounting for the captured windows; it does not prove that the unmatched instruction bytes were valid under the program version deployed at those slots.

## 6SFB and 7rWY instruction layouts

The captured 6SFB pool window is 2025-12-15 00:12:39–00:12:45 UTC; 7rWY is 2025-12-05 08:29:48–08:29:52 UTC. The first-party PumpSwap IDL snapshot from 2025-11-15 and the official SDK `1.11.0` published 2025-11-17 both specify `buy` and `buy_exact_quote_in` with two `u64` arguments plus `track_volume: OptionBool` (one boolean), and `sell` with two `u64` arguments. The corresponding documented buy wire shape is 25 bytes including the 8-byte discriminator. The corrected audit finds 18 of 25 6SFB instructions and 29 of 41 7rWY instructions do not match that shape: 24-, 26-, and 32-byte data variants occur, and one 6SFB buy has 24 accounts where the snapshot lists 23 (the extra key is Rent). Their first two `u64` values still reconcile to the decoded trade events.

The official [`pump-public-docs` history](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/commits) records the Nov. 15, 2025 IDL change, then the Jan. 9, 2026 creator-rewards IDL change; it shows no intervening public-docs commit in that interval. That is bounded public-history evidence, not proof that no private/pre-release code existed or what a deployed program accepted. The available dated SDK/IDL sources do not assign semantics to the missing/extra bytes or Rent account. Do not normalize the data as an omitted false flag, padding, or another ABI. Keep those captures unresolved pending a source tied to the deployed program or another dated official serialization definition.

## F4 trailing 16 bytes

F4’s 61 captured Buy/Sell events are dated 2026-02-13 19:55:01–19:55:07 UTC and each has a 16-byte all-zero suffix after the schema known at the time. Official PumpSwap SDK `1.13.0` was published 2026-01-09; its bundled event IDL has 30 BuyEvent fields and 23 SellEvent fields, with no cashback tail fields. The official SDK `1.14.0` was published 2026-02-17, after the observed trades; its event layouts add exactly `cashback_fee_basis_points: u64` and `cashback: u64`. The official [Feb. 17 `feat: cashback update` commit](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/commit/82dacacf15ca93dc0444ab38714f2226210a0a3d) documents cashback as a backwards-compatible update. This makes the later layout structurally consistent with the bytes, but it cannot establish that those fields had that meaning on Feb. 13. Preserve the dated-schema gap; do not decode the suffix as cashback solely because it is zero and later named fields fit.

The first-party [commit history](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/commits) identifies the Nov. 15, Jan. 9, and Feb. 17 schema changes. The reviewed pre-peak npm tarball and IDL hashes, the 1.14 post-peak hashes, and the raw window hashes are pinned in the private [follow-up manifest](../../../.scout-data/diagnostics/pumpswap-wire-source-followup-2026-10-14/manifest.json). The relevant source captures are also described in the existing [legacy instruction-wire report](2026-10-14-pumpswap-legacy-instruction-wire-research.md) and [cashback schema audit](2026-10-14-solana-pumpswap-buyback-schema.md).

## Evidence boundary

The public source review used official repository history/commit pages and the previously captured official npm registry metadata and tarballs; no chain RPC, price provider, or database calls were made for this follow-up. No match between an SDK package and the historical deployed program binary was established. The corrected replay’s scopes remain the captured pools/windows only, not all PumpSwap activity or all historical ABIs.
